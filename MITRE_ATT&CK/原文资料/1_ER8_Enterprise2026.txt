NEW FOR 2026
What's New in Enterprise 2026
Total Evaluation Score (TES) Framework

For the first time, ATT&CK Evaluations introduces a unified quantitative scoring system. TES combines detection quality (DQI) and protection quality (PQI) into a single comparable metric on a 0–2.0 scale. This moves beyond binary "detected / not detected" results to measure alert quality, analyst precision, platform speed, block timing, and false positive performance — all weighted by technique criticality.

A complete methodology document with full TES scoring components is available at Full Methodology Specification.

Transparent Source-of-Action Attribution

Every TES score is published with a modifier showing who or what produced the quality:

MODIFIER	WHO OPERATES IT	OPERATIONAL MODEL
(P) Platform	Fully automated platform	Customer SOC team investigates from automated alerts
(P/AI) Platform + AI	AI-augmented automation	AI enriches alerts; analysts make final decisions
(S/H) Service + Humans	MDR/MSSP human analysts	Analysts validate, enrich, and escalate complete incidents
(S/AI) Service + AI SOC	Autonomous AI operations	AI performs analyst-equivalent investigation and recommendations
Mixed	Hybrid	Combination of platform automation, human analysts, and AI
Unified Platform and Managed Services Evaluation

Enterprise 2026 eliminates the historical separation between "Enterprise" and "Managed Services" rounds. EDR, XDR, MDR, MSSP, SIEM, and AI SOC vendors are all evaluated on the same adversary scenarios using the same quality metrics. Source-of-Action modifiers provide execution transparency without requiring separate tracks.

Vendor Tooling Declaration

All participating vendors must declare product category tags ensuring customers understand exactly what was tested. Tags: EDR, XDR, Cloud Security, Identity Protection, MDR Service, Network Detection, Managed Service / MSSP, SIEM Integration, Endpoint Protection. Multiple tags are permitted.

EVALUATION SCENARIOS
Threat Context & Scenario Design
Threat Overview

The 2026 ATT&CK® Evaluations reflect the current enterprise threat landscape, where financially motivated intrusions dominate overall activity and state-sponsored operations continue to pose significant risk — particularly in cloud and infrastructure-focused environments.

Adversaries increasingly rely on abuse of trust rather than software exploitation, leveraging user interaction, valid credentials, and legitimate administrative capabilities to gain and maintain access. These intrusions are characterized by living-off-the-land techniques, minimal bespoke tooling, and execution paths that blend into normal enterprise operations. Effective detection and response therefore depend on behavioral context, correlation across systems, and understanding attacker intent — not isolated indicators.

💰
Financially Motivated
Crimeware-as-a-service, double-extortion operations, credential abuse — high-tempo intrusions dominating enterprise incident response
🏛️
State-Sponsored
PRC cyber espionage operations targeting diversified infrastructure — persistent, deliberate, identity-based lateral movement across hybrid environments
🎭
Shared Tradecraft
Both threat types increasingly use living-off-the-land, valid credentials, and legitimate tools — making behavioral correlation the essential detection primitive
Scenario Representation

The two scenarios together reflect the prevailing threat balance — the majority of enterprise intrusions are cybercriminal in origin, while state-sponsored actors continue to drive innovation in infrastructure exploitation.

SCENARIO 1 · ENDPOINT FOCUSED
Financially Motivated Tradecraft
● Registration closed

Crimeware-as-a-Service Operation

This scenario reflects the layered, role-based economy of cybercriminal operations, where specialized actors collaborate across distinct phases of an intrusion without operating as a unified team. It begins with targeted social engineering to harvest credentials, which are then monetized and passed to downstream actors who leverage that access to compromise victim environments and execute on their objectives. Each role in the chain is optimized for results, reflecting how the commoditization of cybercrime has made intrusions faster, harder to attribute, and more accessible to a broader range of adversaries.

Scenario Focus: An endpoint-focused intrusion tracing the crimeware-as-a-service lifecycle, from social engineering-driven credential theft, to environment compromise, lateral movement, data exfiltration, and destructive impact, with living-off-the-land techniques employed throughout to evade detection.

Ideal for: EDR platforms, SIEM integrations, endpoint protection vendors, managed services focused on host-based security
SCENARIO 2 · ENTERPRISE FOCUSED
PRC Espionage Group Tradecraft
● Registration closed

PRC Cyber Espionage Group

A technically advanced PRC cyber-espionage actor that poses a persistent threat to organizations worldwide. The group has operationalized zero-day and n-day vulnerabilities, and exploited internet-facing edge devices and remote access infrastructure to gain footholds. Environmental adaptability, custom payload development, living-off-the-land behaviors, and legitimate cloud services are leveraged to blend into enterprise activity. The group's tradecraft broadly represents how PRC state-sponsored actors operate, sharing tools, techniques, and infrastructure across a well-resourced offensive cyber ecosystem. The convergence complicates attribution and makes the threat relevant to organizations across sectors and geographies.

Scenario Focus: An enterprise-wide, evasive intrusion featuring exploitation of diversified infrastructure, deployment of custom and open-source tooling, and abuse of legitimate cloud services.

Ideal for: XDR platforms, identity protection solutions, MDR/MSSP providers, enterprise-wide visibility offerings
Evaluation Intent

ATT&CK Evaluations are designed to assess how security platforms and services detect, correlate, and respond to modern, trust-abusing behaviors. The evaluation emphasizes three core capabilities:

BEHAVIOR-BASED DETECTION
Identifying adversary activity from behavioral patterns rather than signatures or isolated indicators — what the adversary did, not just which tool they used
MULTI-EVENT CORRELATION
Connecting related events across time, systems, and domains into coherent attack narratives — reducing analyst burden while improving detection fidelity
SIGNAL VS. NOISE DISCRIMINATION
Distinguishing malicious activity from legitimate administrative or user behavior — especially critical where adversaries deliberately blend into normal operations
Technique Scope

The Technique Scope defines which ATT&CK techniques and sub-techniques are in scope for evaluation and how parent-child relationships are handled during scoring. This structure ensures precise, actionable detections and avoids ambiguity when evaluating security controls against adversary behaviors.

Key principles governing scope interpretation:

CONCEPT	HOW IT WORKS
Parent techniques	Encompass additional adversary behaviors, implementations, and variations beyond what is captured by any specific sub-technique. When a parent is in scope, the evaluation assesses the broader behavior category.
Sub-techniques	Provide a more specific, detailed breakdown of particular adversary methods. When a sub-technique is in scope, scoring is evaluated against that specific implementation.
Scope boundary rule	If an adversary behavior does not fit neatly into a specific sub-technique but aligns with the general concept of the parent, it remains in scope under the parent technique. This prevents adversaries from evading evaluation scope through minor implementation variations.

EVALUATION STRUCTURE
What Gets Evaluated
Detections Testing

Protections are disabled so the full adversary attack chain executes. Your solution demonstrates complete visibility. Evaluated on:

DIMENSION	WHAT'S MEASURED	SCORING COMPONENT
Detection Coverage	Alert quality per ATT&CK technique — does your alert provide technique-specific details, ATT&CK mapping, and all 7 context elements (WHO, WHAT, WHEN, WHERE, HOW, WHY, Recommended Action)?	DC tier → Weighted_DC → DQI
Detection Precision	Analyst efficiency — finding real threats without false positives, consolidating related activity into actionable cases rather than fragmented alerts	DP → DQI
Detection Speed	Elapsed time from technique execution to first automated platform alert. Human analyst escalation time is excluded.	DS → DQI
Protections Testing

Protections are enabled. Tests are stage-gated: once you block, the test ends. Evaluated on:

DIMENSION	WHAT'S MEASURED	SCORING COMPONENT
Protection Coverage	Block timing in the attack chain (primary) + context quality of the block alert (secondary). Blocking before the first critical stage scores PC-3; blocking at the terminal stage after all critical objectives scores PC-1.	PC tier → Weighted_PC → PQI
Protection Precision	False positive validation: can you block threats without disrupting legitimate business activity? Each incorrectly blocked benign activity reduces your PP score.	PP → PQI
False Positive Validation

Both scenarios include dedicated false positive testing with legitimate business activities — IT administration scripts, approved software downloads, remote desktop connections, cloud storage access, network file transfers, and data processing tools. Solutions that over-block benign activity receive lower precision scores that feed directly into PQI.

PREPARATION GUIDANCE
How to Prepare Before Your Evaluation

These preparation steps will materially improve your TES scores. Start before your execution week — some require architecture decisions that take weeks to implement.

Detection Coverage (DC)
DQI

DC is scored at the behavior level — one DC score per ATT&CK technique regardless of how many alerts you generate.

Ensure alerts include all 7 context elements: WHO, WHAT (ATT&CK mapped), WHEN, WHERE, HOW, WHY, Recommended Action
DC-3 requires explicit ATT&CK technique mapping — tactic-level only scores DC-2
Test your alert quality against specific ATT&CK techniques in Scenario 1 before your execution week
Detection Precision (DP)
DQI

DP rewards consolidation. One incident case covering 50 behaviors scores far higher than 50 separate cases for the same behaviors.

Configure your correlation rules to consolidate related adversary activity into unified incident cases
Test false positive rate against the business activity types used in FP validation testing
Reduce case fragmentation — the case-count penalty is significant
Detection Speed (DS)
DQI

DS measures time from technique execution to first automated platform alert. Sub-15-minute = Real-Time tier (score: 1.0).

If you're a cloud platform, measure actual backend detection time vs. UI rendering time — they differ
Plan to submit backend timestamps during the results review period if UI latency is a factor
Identify and reduce pipeline bottlenecks (API polling intervals, batch ingestion delays)
Protection Coverage (PC) & Timing
PQI

Block timing is the primary PC tier determinant. Blocking before the first critical stage scores PIW 1.0× (maximum); blocking at the terminal stage scores PIW 0.25× (minimum).

Block alerts should also include the 7 context elements — full context at Stage 1–2 → PC-3; partial → PC-2
If using "observe before block" strategy, document it in advance with supporting telemetry plan
Test block triggers against the specific technique categories in Scenario 1
📋

The Full Methodology Specification contains complete scoring formulas, all 7 context element definitions, tier assignment matrices, and the cloud latency timestamp submission process. Reading it before your execution week is the highest-leverage preparation activity.

EVALUATION SCENARIOS
Threat Context & Scenario Design
Threat Overview

The 2026 ATT&CK® Evaluations reflect the current enterprise threat landscape, where financially motivated intrusions dominate overall activity and state-sponsored operations continue to pose significant risk — particularly in cloud and infrastructure-focused environments.

Adversaries increasingly rely on abuse of trust rather than software exploitation, leveraging user interaction, valid credentials, and legitimate administrative capabilities to gain and maintain access. These intrusions are characterized by living-off-the-land techniques, minimal bespoke tooling, and execution paths that blend into normal enterprise operations. Effective detection and response therefore depend on behavioral context, correlation across systems, and understanding attacker intent — not isolated indicators.

💰
Financially Motivated
Crimeware-as-a-service, double-extortion operations, credential abuse — high-tempo intrusions dominating enterprise incident response
🏛️
State-Sponsored
PRC cyber espionage operations targeting diversified infrastructure — persistent, deliberate, identity-based lateral movement across hybrid environments
🎭
Shared Tradecraft
Both threat types increasingly use living-off-the-land, valid credentials, and legitimate tools — making behavioral correlation the essential detection primitive
Scenario Representation

The two scenarios together reflect the prevailing threat balance — the majority of enterprise intrusions are cybercriminal in origin, while state-sponsored actors continue to drive innovation in infrastructure exploitation.

SCENARIO 1 · ENDPOINT FOCUSED
Financially Motivated Tradecraft
● Registration closed

Crimeware-as-a-Service Operation

This scenario reflects the layered, role-based economy of cybercriminal operations, where specialized actors collaborate across distinct phases of an intrusion without operating as a unified team. It begins with targeted social engineering to harvest credentials, which are then monetized and passed to downstream actors who leverage that access to compromise victim environments and execute on their objectives. Each role in the chain is optimized for results, reflecting how the commoditization of cybercrime has made intrusions faster, harder to attribute, and more accessible to a broader range of adversaries.

Scenario Focus: An endpoint-focused intrusion tracing the crimeware-as-a-service lifecycle, from social engineering-driven credential theft, to environment compromise, lateral movement, data exfiltration, and destructive impact, with living-off-the-land techniques employed throughout to evade detection.

Ideal for: EDR platforms, SIEM integrations, endpoint protection vendors, managed services focused on host-based security
SCENARIO 2 · ENTERPRISE FOCUSED
PRC Espionage Group Tradecraft
● Registration closed

PRC Cyber Espionage Group

A technically advanced PRC cyber-espionage actor that poses a persistent threat to organizations worldwide. The group has operationalized zero-day and n-day vulnerabilities, and exploited internet-facing edge devices and remote access infrastructure to gain footholds. Environmental adaptability, custom payload development, living-off-the-land behaviors, and legitimate cloud services are leveraged to blend into enterprise activity. The group's tradecraft broadly represents how PRC state-sponsored actors operate, sharing tools, techniques, and infrastructure across a well-resourced offensive cyber ecosystem. The convergence complicates attribution and makes the threat relevant to organizations across sectors and geographies.

Scenario Focus: An enterprise-wide, evasive intrusion featuring exploitation of diversified infrastructure, deployment of custom and open-source tooling, and abuse of legitimate cloud services.

Ideal for: XDR platforms, identity protection solutions, MDR/MSSP providers, enterprise-wide visibility offerings
Evaluation Intent

ATT&CK Evaluations are designed to assess how security platforms and services detect, correlate, and respond to modern, trust-abusing behaviors. The evaluation emphasizes three core capabilities:

BEHAVIOR-BASED DETECTION
Identifying adversary activity from behavioral patterns rather than signatures or isolated indicators — what the adversary did, not just which tool they used
MULTI-EVENT CORRELATION
Connecting related events across time, systems, and domains into coherent attack narratives — reducing analyst burden while improving detection fidelity
SIGNAL VS. NOISE DISCRIMINATION
Distinguishing malicious activity from legitimate administrative or user behavior — especially critical where adversaries deliberately blend into normal operations
Technique Scope

The Technique Scope defines which ATT&CK techniques and sub-techniques are in scope for evaluation and how parent-child relationships are handled during scoring. This structure ensures precise, actionable detections and avoids ambiguity when evaluating security controls against adversary behaviors.

Key principles governing scope interpretation:

CONCEPT	HOW IT WORKS
Parent techniques	Encompass additional adversary behaviors, implementations, and variations beyond what is captured by any specific sub-technique. When a parent is in scope, the evaluation assesses the broader behavior category.
Sub-techniques	Provide a more specific, detailed breakdown of particular adversary methods. When a sub-technique is in scope, scoring is evaluated against that specific implementation.
Scope boundary rule	If an adversary behavior does not fit neatly into a specific sub-technique but aligns with the general concept of the parent, it remains in scope under the parent technique. This prevents adversaries from evading evaluation scope through minor implementation variations.

EVALUATION STRUCTURE
What Gets Evaluated
Detections Testing

Protections are disabled so the full adversary attack chain executes. Your solution demonstrates complete visibility. Evaluated on:

DIMENSION	WHAT'S MEASURED	SCORING COMPONENT
Detection Coverage	Alert quality per ATT&CK technique — does your alert provide technique-specific details, ATT&CK mapping, and all 7 context elements (WHO, WHAT, WHEN, WHERE, HOW, WHY, Recommended Action)?	DC tier → Weighted_DC → DQI
Detection Precision	Analyst efficiency — finding real threats without false positives, consolidating related activity into actionable cases rather than fragmented alerts	DP → DQI
Detection Speed	Elapsed time from technique execution to first automated platform alert. Human analyst escalation time is excluded.	DS → DQI
Protections Testing

Protections are enabled. Tests are stage-gated: once you block, the test ends. Evaluated on:

DIMENSION	WHAT'S MEASURED	SCORING COMPONENT
Protection Coverage	Block timing in the attack chain (primary) + context quality of the block alert (secondary). Blocking before the first critical stage scores PC-3; blocking at the terminal stage after all critical objectives scores PC-1.	PC tier → Weighted_PC → PQI
Protection Precision	False positive validation: can you block threats without disrupting legitimate business activity? Each incorrectly blocked benign activity reduces your PP score.	PP → PQI
False Positive Validation

Both scenarios include dedicated false positive testing with legitimate business activities — IT administration scripts, approved software downloads, remote desktop connections, cloud storage access, network file transfers, and data processing tools. Solutions that over-block benign activity receive lower precision scores that feed directly into PQI.

PREPARATION GUIDANCE
How to Prepare Before Your Evaluation

These preparation steps will materially improve your TES scores. Start before your execution week — some require architecture decisions that take weeks to implement.

Detection Coverage (DC)
DQI

DC is scored at the behavior level — one DC score per ATT&CK technique regardless of how many alerts you generate.

Ensure alerts include all 7 context elements: WHO, WHAT (ATT&CK mapped), WHEN, WHERE, HOW, WHY, Recommended Action
DC-3 requires explicit ATT&CK technique mapping — tactic-level only scores DC-2
Test your alert quality against specific ATT&CK techniques in Scenario 1 before your execution week
Detection Precision (DP)
DQI

DP rewards consolidation. One incident case covering 50 behaviors scores far higher than 50 separate cases for the same behaviors.

Configure your correlation rules to consolidate related adversary activity into unified incident cases
Test false positive rate against the business activity types used in FP validation testing
Reduce case fragmentation — the case-count penalty is significant
Detection Speed (DS)
DQI

DS measures time from technique execution to first automated platform alert. Sub-15-minute = Real-Time tier (score: 1.0).

If you're a cloud platform, measure actual backend detection time vs. UI rendering time — they differ
Plan to submit backend timestamps during the results review period if UI latency is a factor
Identify and reduce pipeline bottlenecks (API polling intervals, batch ingestion delays)
Protection Coverage (PC) & Timing
PQI

Block timing is the primary PC tier determinant. Blocking before the first critical stage scores PIW 1.0× (maximum); blocking at the terminal stage scores PIW 0.25× (minimum).

Block alerts should also include the 7 context elements — full context at Stage 1–2 → PC-3; partial → PC-2
If using "observe before block" strategy, document it in advance with supporting telemetry plan
Test block triggers against the specific technique categories in Scenario 1
